Date and time over the recorded video

The video recorder (Drawer: Record → Video) gets two settings. With the switch on, the current date and time is shown over the picture in one of the four corners, the way a camcorder or a dash cam does it.

What is asked

  • recordDateTimeOverEnabled - a switch
  • recordDateTimeOverPosition - a radio: Top Left, Top Right, Bottom Left, Bottom Right

What is built (phase 1)

  • The settings uxuiState.RecordVideoSettings (persisted), the twin of PlayVideoSettings; a gear button on the recorder card opens the panel.
  • Both recorders show the stamp over the preview: RecordVideo.tsx (iOS / Android, Vision Camera 4) and RecordVideo.web.tsx (browser MediaRecorder).
  • Web: the stamp is written into the file. iOS / Android: the player of the app draws it from the times kept in the post.
  • Seen on a real Android phone (Samsung A33): panel, switch, radio, stamp over the camera picture.

The one hard point

On the web the stamp can be written into the file itself. On iOS / Android the camera writes the file in hardware and nothing can be drawn into it on the way: it needs either a second pass over the file (new native code) or a stamp that the app shows when it plays the video. Phase 1 took the second way (answer Q1); the first is phase 2. See Ways.

The settings

New object uxuiState.RecordVideoSettings - per device, persisted by redux-persist, like PlayVideoSettings. No SQL.

Rules of the panel

  • Opened by a new gear button on the recorder card, next to the camera flip button (the same idea as the gear of the player).
  • The row under the picture, from the left: Record button, switch Sound on / Sound off, camera flip, gear at the right edge - one line also on a phone. While a recording runs Record gives its place to the line Pause / Stop & save under the row.
  • The floating "+" button of the application is not shown on this route (fabRoutes.ts): it covered the radio.
  • The card has no title "Record video" above the camera picture any more: the application bar already says it.
  • Both controls are locked while a recording runs (recording, paused, switching the sound, saving): one video has one position.
  • A state saved by an older app version has no such object: every reader goes through selectRecordVideoSettings (defaults, an unknown position becomes bottomRight).
  • Reset (a plain text button, no border) puts both back to the defaults.

The look of the stamp

    
    
      
  • Text YYYY-MM-DD HH:mm:ss, local time of the device, 24 hours, changes every second (question Q2).
    • 
      
  • White monospace text with a dark shadow, no box: readable on a light and on a dark picture.
    • 
      
  • Height of the text = 4 % of the short side of the picture; margin = 3 % of the width, 4 % of the height. One pure function gives the place for the preview, the file and the player, so the three always agree.
    • 
      
  • The red time badge of the recorder stands top left; with the stamp at Top Left it moves to the top right.
    • 
      
  • In the player the place is counted from the player box (16 : 9), not from the picture inside it: a portrait video has the stamp in the black side bar. Known limit of phase 1.
    • 
      
  • The front camera preview is mirrored; the stamp is never mirrored.
    • 
    

  






  
How it works

  
One settings object, one pure helper, three places that draw.

  

    
  

  

    
Web the stamp is written into the file

      
    
        
  • Today: camera stream → MediaRecorder. New, only while the switch is on: camera stream → hidden <canvas> (each frame is drawn, then the text) → canvas.captureStream() + the microphone track → MediaRecorder.
    • 
        
  • The canvas has the size of the camera picture (1280 × 720 asked today), so the quality is the same.
    • 
        
  • The visible preview stays the <video> tag with a text above it (a mirrored canvas would mirror the text).
    • 
        
  • Frames are taken with requestVideoFrameCallback, a timer where the browser has none.
    • 
        
  • The sound switch ("Sound on" / "Sound off"), pause and the 1 hour limit keep working: they act on the same recorder.
    • 
        
  • Switch off = the old direct path, not one line changes for it.
    • 
      

    
iOS / Android phase 1: the app shows it

      
    
        
  • The file is recorded as today (hardware H.264). The post keeps when each part started and every pause.
    • 
        
  • The player of the app draws the same text at the same place: the time = start of the part + the position in the video, pauses and the gap of a sound switch counted in. A video of several parts stays correct.
    • 
        
  • The player gets the times from the post: the result card after a recording (Play) passes them; the play route takes them as &stamp=<JSON> (buildPlayRoute({ dateTimeOver })).
    • 
        
  • No new native library, no new development build, no waiting after Stop.
    • 
        
  • Limit: a file taken out of the app (shared, uploaded and opened in another program) has no stamp.
    • 
      

    
iOS / Android phase 2: into the file

      
    
        
  • After Stop the file is written once more with the stamp: iOS AVFoundation (video composition), Android Media3 Transformer with a text overlay (Media3 1.9.0 is already in the app through expo-video).
    • 
        
  • That is a small local native module (Swift + Kotlin) and a new development build.
    • 
        
  • Cost: the user waits after Stop (estimate 0.3-1 × the length of the video, to be measured), battery, one more encode of the picture.
    • 
        
  • Starts with a spike S0 on two real phones; phase 1 already works meanwhile and the post simply changes to burnedIn: true.
    •

Reading the second sample

    
The times are stored in UTC and shown in the local time of the device (here UTC+1). parts has one entry per file, in the order of the files. Second 30 of part 1 lies after the pause at second 12, so the player shows 14:03:15 + 30 s + 20 s = 2026-11-12 14:04:05. Part 2 starts 0.7 s after part 1 ended (the gap of the sound switch) and shows its own startedAt. A web post has burnedIn: true and no parts: the player draws nothing. A post without mediaDateTimeOver (every post of today) is played as today.

Tests and limits

  

    
Jest: 159 tests of __tests__/media pass

      
    
        
  • recordVideoSettings.test.ts: defaults, an old persisted state, a wrong position, reset.
    • 
        
  • dateTimeOver.test.ts: the text, the four places for landscape and portrait, the clock time with pauses and parts (the sample above is a case).
    • 
        
  • The panel is tested inside recordVideoNative.test.tsx: the radio is disabled while the switch is off; everything is locked while recording; Reset; the time badge moves.
    • 
        
  • recordVideoWeb / recordVideoNative: switch off = the path of today; switch on = canvas path (fake canvas) / the times in the post.
    • 
        
  • videoPlayerViewWeb, mediaPlayer: the stamp shows only for burnedIn: false, each file gets its part, an audio or another source gets none.
    • 
        
  • Not covered by a test: the stamp inside the native VideoPlayerView.tsx (expo-video); the whole Jest run of the project was not repeated, only the media folder.
    • 
      

    
What a test cannot show

      
    
        
  • A real camera: the stamp in a real file must be looked at in Chrome, Firefox and Safari, and on the phones.
    • 
        
  • Web, tab in the background: browsers slow down a hidden tab, the picture of that stretch can stand still (the sound goes on). To be checked; said in the README.
    • 
        
  • Web, weak computer: drawing 30 frames a second costs processor time. To be measured on a 1 hour recording.
    • 
        
  • The clock is the clock of the device: a wrong device clock gives a wrong stamp.
    •

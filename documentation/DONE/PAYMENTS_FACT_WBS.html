Payments Fact – Work Breakdown Structure

W1 FACT Payments. Scope: ONE bank transaction input – the document PMPaymentFact (Form · List · Header · Lines) with the tables project_payment_fact_header / project_payment_fact_lines, the lines taken from the posted payment plan (W1 PLAN PAYMENTS), and the register cash_account_ledger_movements with the rests of a money account that are recalculated when an exchange rate changes. Version 1 (2026-10-23) was analysis only (the owner’s instruction “no code”); version 2 (2026-10-23) is built after the owner’s answers (section 9, status in section 12). Sources: the Google Doc “W1 FACT Payments” (read 2026-10-23, no comments in it) and the code as it stands on 2026-10-23. Version 2.

1. Goal

W1 PLAN PAYMENTS (built, PART 37) answers “when do we expect the money to move”. W1 FACT answers “when did it really move”. The business need is one bank transaction input: the accountant enters ONE bank statement line (one money account, one payee, one direction, one date) and states what it paid – and one payment can pay several contracts of the same partner, under several budget items (and, because the plan lines carry them, several tasks / business units). Everything that describes a line (task, contract, budget item, business unit, currency, sums) is taken from the posted payment plan (“GET FROM PLAN”) and may be corrected.

A posted payment fact writes one row per line into the register cash_account_ledger_movements. From these rows the rest (balance) of every money account on every day is derived. Because the rest in the accounting and budget currency depends on the exchange rate of the day, a change of an exchange rate makes the rests recalculate from date to date (the owner’s “!!!!!!” note).

Hard constraint (as for every feature of this app): nothing existing changes. A project that never enters a payment fact behaves exactly as before; the payment plan, the posted plan payments, the task lines, the register project_status_movements, Monitor01/02/03 and the documents (fact input, invoices, receipts, time sheet) are not touched. The payment fact reads the posted plan; nothing existing reads the payment fact in W1 (a Cash flow report that compares plan and fact is a later round).

Rules

  • Header ⇄ lines. The payee and the direction are on the header; every line’s contract must belong to that payee, every line’s budget item must be of the payments genus with the direction of the header, a person is only paid (Outbound). A draft may change them (the lines that no longer fit are listed, not silently changed); a posted fact is read only (Unpost first).
  • GET FROM PLAN, header. Choosing a posted plan payment fills paymentFactPartner | Person and paymentFactDirection (and, when the money account is empty, nothing else – the account is the accountant’s own information).
  • GET FROM PLAN, lines. A button on the lines opens a list of the posted plan payments of the header’s payee and direction (RPC payment_fact_plan_candidates); each shows task, contract, budget item, planned date and the open amount = plan sum − the lines of posted payment facts that point to it. A check box each → Add (RPC payment_fact_add_from_plan) makes one line per payment with the attributes copied (R5) and sumForContract = the open amount. A plan payment can be taken again for the rest while something is open; paying more than open is a warning, not an error (the fact may differ from the plan).
  • Sums. sumForContract is typed and WITH VAT (cash). sumForAccounting / sumForBudget are calculated from it with the rate of paymentFactDate (the same rule as the plan: fx_convert, latest earlier rate, no rate = empty cell + the Errors column says why; Post is refused). The VAT-basis switch does not apply (same pitfall as the plan).
  • Post (one transaction, all or nothing, answer { status, lines, problems[] }): needs number, date, money account, payee, direction, ≥ 1 line, every line complete (task, contract, budget item, currency, sum > 0, the converted sums), the user owns every project of the lines. It writes one movement per line, queues (moneyAccount, paymentFactDate), and takes the per-account lock (pg_advisory_xact_lock on the account) so that two Posts into one account do not interleave. Unpost deletes the movements and queues the same day. A draft can be deleted; a posted fact cannot.
  • Rests (R6). The worker adds, per account, currency and sum kind, the daily turnover of the posted movements and the running rest: the rest after day D = the opening balance + the sum of all turnovers up to D. Only the days from the queue’s fromDay onward are rewritten (the rest of an earlier day cannot change). Posting or unposting with an old date therefore re-rolls the rests from that date to today.
  • Rate change (R7) – “recalculate the rests from date to date”. A statement trigger on currencyExchangeRateTable (insert / update / delete of a row, also a change of startingDate, rateType, rateBase) records the span of days the rate rules: from = its startingDate (old and new, the smaller), to = the startingDate of the next row of the same currency / type / base (open end = today). For every posted movement whose day is in the span and whose conversion goes through that currency (a row quoted against another base: every movement of the span, because the cross rate changes) the worker recalculates sumForAccounting / sumForBudget from sumForContract, stores the new fxSnapshot and recalculatedAt, and then rebuilds the rests from the first changed day to today for the affected accounts. sumForContract (what the bank moved) never changes. Without a rate for a day the old value stays and the movement is listed by cash_ledger_verify.
  • Verify. cash_ledger_verify(account) recomputes everything from the movements and compares it with the stored rests / sums (maintenance function without an owner filter, like roi_verify); cash_ledger_rebuild rebuilds the rests from the movements (they are the only table that can always be rebuilt).
  • Who reads what. The client reads the movements and rests of the accounts of its organization (RLS by rowOrganizationGUID of the account); it never writes them.

6. Sequence, dependencies and acceptance criteria

  1. 1 Decisions – Q1 (account currency) and Q2 (header scope) change the tables, so they are answered before 3.
  2. 2 Money account catalog and 3.1–3.2 tables first; 3.3–3.4 then; 3.5–3.7 (rests, rate trigger, cron) after the movements exist. 4 (pure rules) is independent of 3 and can start at once.
  3. 5 Screens after 3.3 + 4; the tab (5.5) and menu items (5.6) last.
  4. 6 Quality – tests are written with each step, as always.

Acceptance criteria

  • The task window → Fact has the 2nd-level tab Payments with the sub-tabs Outbound · Inbound listing the payment facts of the task; Drawer → Documents has Payment facts; Catalogs has Money accounts. A project that never uses them shows no change anywhere else.
  • A payment fact is ONE bank transaction: header with money account, payee (partner or person), direction, number, date. Get from plan fills the payee and direction from a posted plan payment, and adds lines from the posted plan payments of that payee and direction.
  • The RULE: one fact can hold lines with different contracts of the payee, different budget items, business units, tasks and projects; a line of another payee’s contract, or a budget item of the wrong direction, is refused.
  • Only posted plan payments are offered; each shows its open amount; taking a payment twice offers only what is still open; paying more is a warning.
  • The budget item and the contract of a line can be edited on a draft; the sums are WITH VAT; sumForAccounting / sumForBudget follow the rate of the date of the header (no rate = the cell is empty and says why, Post refused).
  • Post writes one signed movement per line into cash_account_ledger_movements in one transaction; Post twice (Unpost between) gives the same rows; an incomplete document refuses Post with a list; Unpost removes the movements; a posted fact cannot be changed or deleted; the draft can.
  • The rest of an account on a day = the opening balance + all movements up to that day; posting or unposting with an old date corrects the rests from that date; two simultaneous Posts into one account give the same rests as the same Posts one after the other.
  • Rate change: adding / changing / deleting an exchange rate row recalculates sumForAccounting / sumForBudget of the posted movements in the span the rate rules, and the rests of the affected accounts from the first changed day to today; sumForContract does not change; a rate that no movement uses changes nothing; cash_ledger_verify reports no difference afterwards.
  • Another organization cannot read the documents, the movements or the rests; the client cannot write the movements or the rests.
  • test_sql.sh passes (install twice, delete, install); the new Docker test and the new Jest tests pass; no new FAIL against the Jest baseline.

SQL checks the Docker test will make (tests/96)

  • create / header guards (payee xor person, person only Outbound, direction, status) / RLS of two organizations;
  • plan candidates: only posted plan payments, open amount after a partial fact, after Unpost, after the plan payment is unposted;
  • one fact with 3 lines (2 contracts, 2 budget items, 2 tasks) posts, writes 3 movements with the right signs; wrong payee’s contract refused;
  • rests: opening balance, two days, a back-dated Post, an Unpost, a second account untouched; the rest equals cash_ledger_verify;
  • rate change: a new row, a changed ratio, a deleted row, a moved startingDate, a cross-base row; only the span is recalculated; rests equal the verify; twice = same rows;
  • currency of an account with movements cannot change; account with movements cannot be deleted; the task’s delete cascades lines (and refuses a posted fact – see Q6).

8. Questions for the owner (the answers change the design)

#QuestionRecommendation
Q1What is the money account balance in? The register of the Google Doc has three sums (budget, contract, accounting). A bank account has its OWN currency (e.g. EUR) and the bank statement shows the balance in it – which may be none of the three (a USD contract paid from a EUR account, project currencies PLN / GBP). A fourth amount is needed to reproduce the bank’s balance.Add sumForMoneyAccount (+ moneyAccountCurrency) to the line and the movement: typed on the line (the bank’s real debit) or converted at the rate of the date when left empty. The rests are kept per account currency (the real balance) and per the three sums. Without it the rest can only be shown when the account currency equals the contract currency.
Q2Scope of the header: one project or the whole organization? The table name says project_payment_fact_header, but “1 payment → N contracts / N budget items” pays one partner for several tasks – and the plan lines already belong to different projects.Organization scope (like the vendor invoice header), every line carries its own project and task; Post needs the ownership of every project. The task tab shows the facts that have a line on the task.
Q3Opening balance and period close. Does the rest start from an opening balance entered on the money account (a date + an amount), and must the days before it be locked?Yes: opening date + balance on the account; a Post before the opening date is refused. No period close in W1.

11. Risks

  • Recalculation cost. A rate row quoted against another base touches every movement in its span; the worker runs in batches and has a bench (3.9). Supabase’s 8 s limit for authenticated applies to the RPCs, not to the cron worker, but the manual “Refresh rests” button calls it – keep the call bounded (the pitfall of AS MATERIALIZED).
  • Mixed currencies in one rest. Lines of different projects have different accounting / budget currencies; the rests are therefore kept per currency code (section 4) – without Q1 a single “balance” of an account does not exist for the three sums.
  • Back-dated Post. A Post 2 years back re-rolls 2 years of rests; the queue stores the first day only, so many back-dated Posts cost one pass.
  • Two implementations of one rule. The converted sums exist in SQL and in TypeScript (as in the time sheet): the golden cases test (section 6.1) keeps them equal.
  • The owner’s Supabase is not visible from the build session: the SQL is proven on Docker Postgres 15 only; pg_cron of the real Supabase is checked there.

13. Decisions taken while building

  • Rest model. One row per day that has movements, per sum kind (account · contract · accounting · budget) and currency code, key (organization, account, kind, currency, day); the rest = the opening balance (kind account only) + the running sum of the daily turnovers. Post and Unpost rebuild the rests of that ONE account from the date of the document in the same transaction (under an advisory lock of the account); only the rate changes go through the queue.
  • The span of a rate. A rate row rules [its date, the next row of the same currency / type / base). The trigger queues the span of the old and the new date (a moved date, a changed ratio, a deleted row); a row quoted against another base queues every currency (the cross rates change). The seed of the ECB rates costs nothing: the trigger returns at once while no payment fact was ever posted.
  • What is recalculated. sumForAccounting, sumForBudget and (only when not typed) sumForMoneyAccount of the movements, at the rate types of the line’s project (exchangeRateTypeForAccounting default Default, …Budget default Budget); a sum whose rate is missing keeps its value (cash_ledger_verify lists the differences). A typed amount and sumForContract never change.
  • The line trigger converts, the client does not. The cells that are not editable (accounting, budget, source) arrive by realtime; a line of a plan payment keeps the currency of the payment (the open amount is computed in it).
  • Get from plan. Only POSTED plan payments of projects the user owns; the open amount subtracts the posted facts and the lines of THIS draft; a payment whose payee differs from the document’s is skipped with a reason. An empty document takes the payee (and a missing direction) of the first payment.
  • Speed (10 000 movements, 1 000 days): all the rests 70 ms, the rests from day 500 50 ms, a rate change that rules 9 400 movements 2.5 s, verify 2.4 s – under the 8 s limit of an authenticated call (the button “Refresh rests”).

14. Open points

  • A typed amount of the account is recognised by its difference from the stored one (the client sends the whole rowJSON): two quick edits of one line before the first save came back can take the older amount for a typed one (clear the cell to convert again).
  • The pg_cron worker converts with the base currency EUR unless the calling user’s organization says otherwise (fx_base_currency); an organization with another base currency recalculates with the button of the app. Not exercised on the owner’s Supabase.
  • The Cash flow report (plan vs fact), a project action that posts payment facts, bank statement import, transfers between accounts and FX gain / loss are not built (section 10).

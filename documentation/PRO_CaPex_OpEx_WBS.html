appPM80 for a CFO, a CEO and investors: CapEx and OpEx

Make Project Budget Management (appPM80) look and behave the way a CFO, a CEO, a board / investor and a capital committee expect (CFO = control, CEO = decisions and portfolio, investor = capital efficiency and trust in the numbers; see the three audiences). For the CFO this means: every amount knows whether it is CapEx or OpEx, every report can be cut by it, capital spending is requested, approved, committed, forecast and finally capitalised in a controlled way.

Revised 2026-11-20. The first version (2026-11-17) served the CFO. This revision adds the CEO and investor view: section three audiences, mock-ups of the executive page and the investor KPI strip, work package H (executive and investor layer, H1 - H12), decisions Q13 - Q17 and two new risks. Nothing of the CFO plan (A - G) was removed; its phases were extended.

The good news. The hardest part of the foundation is done: a budget item already carries an IAS 7 activity (operatingActivity / investingActivity / financingActivity) in its key, with a SQL-checked matrix of 14 allowed cells (PART 32), and the VAT basis already separates "VAT that is cost" (the part of VAT that belongs in a capitalised cost). Three audiences, three questions

"Maximum professional" means something different for each reader. The same data serves all three; what changes is the page, the words and the level of detail.

CFO / controller control

Reads for 30 minutes, checks every number.

  • Is the money classified correctly, and does it agree with the books?
  • Budget vs committed vs actual vs forecast, by class, project, fiscal period.
  • Who approved, when, against which limit?
  • What will this do to the P&L (depreciation) and to cash?

Answered by: work packages A - G (Monitor01 by class, Monitor09, capital request, approval, WIP).

CEO decisions

Reads for 2 minutes, wants to know what to decide.

  • How much capital is deployed, committed, still free - and is it going to the strategy?
  • Which projects are red, and what is the money at risk?
  • What waits for my decision (approvals, transfers, over-budget)?
  • Growth spend vs keep-the-lights-on spend: is the split what I intended?

Answered by: H1 executive page, H3 strategic tag, H5 decision queue, D8 / D9 portfolio and exceptions.

Investor / board trust

Reads for 10 minutes a quarter, distrusts anything that moves.

  • How capital-intensive is the business (CapEx / revenue), and is the asset base growing or shrinking (CapEx / depreciation)?
  • What return does the capital earn (NPV, IRR, payback), and did past projects deliver what was promised?
  • Is CapEx guidance for the year still intact (forecast vs range)?
  • Can I trust these numbers? Same number tomorrow, reconciled to the ledger, every definition stated.

Answered by: H2 efficiency KPIs, H6 period snapshot, H7 reconciliation, H9 guidance, H10 track record, F1, H4 read-only view.

How a CFO reads CapEx and OpEx - the rules the app must follow

Orientation, IFRS first. US GAAP has its twins (ASC 360, ASC 350-40, ASC 842). The policy table of this plan makes the thresholds and lives editable per organization.

CapEx capital expenditure

Spending that creates or extends an asset with benefit beyond one year: property, plant and equipment (IAS 16), intangible assets and development cost (IAS 38), directly attributable labour and installation, non-recoverable VAT and duties, borrowing cost of a qualifying asset (IAS 23). Appears in the balance sheet (first as WIP / assets under construction, then as an asset) and in investing cash flow (IAS 7.16); reaches the P&L later as depreciation / amortisation.

OpEx operating expenditure

Spending that is consumed in the period: wages, rent, utilities, repairs and maintenance, licences paid per period, research cost (IAS 38.54), training, audit. Appears in the P&L at once and in operating cash flow. Cash timing and P&L timing are the same (apart from accruals).

Grey zones the app must handle

  • Own labour: capitalise only hours on development tasks of an asset, with evidence.
  • Software: research OpEx, development CapEx (six IAS 38.57 criteria), run costs OpEx.
  • Maintenance vs improvement: a repair is OpEx, an upgrade that extends life or capacity is CapEx.
  • Mixed invoice: one invoice, part asset, part expense: split by % or amount.
  • Financing: loans, lease principal, dividends are neither (the key already has financingActivity).

The classification decision (what the policy encodes)

  1. Is it financing (loan, equity, dividend, lease principal)? → Financing, in neither bucket.
  2. Does it create or extend an asset that gives benefit longer than the capitalisation period (default 12 months)? No → OpEx.
  3. Is it above the capitalisation threshold of the organization (per asset or per project)? No → OpEx (de minimis).
  4. Is it in the research phase of the project (IAS 38)? Yes → OpEx.
  5. Otherwise → CapEx: it collects in WIP until the asset is ready for use; from the in-service date it is depreciated over the useful life.

The budget item gives the default answer; a task line may override it with a reason; the answer is frozen on every posted movement.

Phases - what changes for the CFO, step by step

1 Visible

Result: open the home, choose CapEx, see real numbers; open Monitor09, see budget - committed - actual - paid by class.

Contents: A1 A2 A3 A4 A8 · B1 · C1 C2 C3 · D1 - D5 · E7 · G1 - G5 · H12.

Why first: it needs no new document and no workflow, it uses data that is already posted, and it removes the biggest credibility risk (demo numbers).

2 Control

Result: nothing is bought above the threshold without an approved request; the committee sees EAC, variance, cash flow, NPV; the CEO has one page and a decision queue; an investor gets a read-only view and a frozen, dated pack.

Contents: A7 · B2 - B7 · C4 - C9 · D6 - D10 · F1 F2 · H1 - H6 H8 H9 H11.

Hint: this phase is large; cut it in two releases: 2a = B2 B3 B4 B6 B7 H5 (control), 2b = C4 - C9 D6 - D10 F1 F2 H1 - H4 H6 H8 H9 H11 (reporting and the executive / investor layer).

3 Accounting bridge

Result: finished projects are capitalised with a document; WIP and depreciation forecast are reported; accounting gets a journal CSV.

Contents: A5 A6 · B8 - B10 · C10 - C12 · E1 - E4 · F3 · H7 H10.

4 Appraisal and depth

Result: benefits realisation, borrowing cost, optionally leases.

Contents: E5 E6 · F4.

The minimum that already looks professional = D1 (live numbers) + A1 / A8 (right classes) + C2 first step + D2 (slicer) + D4 (basis line). Everything else makes the app a control tool, not just a report.
The minimum for an investor conversation = the above + H6 (a closed period that does not move) + H2 (defined KPIs) + H4 (read-only access) + H7 (reconciled to the ledger). Without a frozen, reconciled number, the best-looking page is only a demo - an investor asks for these two things first.

Decisions to take (each with a recommended default)

Same style as the other WBS pages: the default is what I would build if you do not answer.

#QuestionRecommended default
Q1Where is the class decided?Budget item = default; task line may override with a reason; a posted movement is frozen (A1, A2, A3).
Q2Which accounting standard?IFRS first; the policy table keeps thresholds and lives so US GAAP or local rules are settings, not code.
Q3Is the app the book of record for fixed assets?No. The ERP stays the statutory register. The app owns the project side: WIP, a capitalisation package, a depreciation forecast, and a journal CSV (phase 3).
Q4Fiscal yearAn organization setting "fiscal year starts in month N" (default January); all fiscal reports use it.
Q5Capitalised own labourOff by default; when on: only hours on tasks of a development stage, loaded rate = rate + burden % (A4, A6, B8).
Q6Capitalisation thresholdOne amount per organization in the budget currency (default 0 = off), overridable per project; below it the item is OpEx.
Q7Approval limitsA matrix class × amount → role; default: project manager up to a limit, then a capital approver, then the administrator.
Q8May a policy change move posted history?No. Only a reclassification document moves posted money, with a reason and an audit row.
Q9Currency of a capital request and of the portfolioRequest in the project budget currency; portfolio and home in the consolidation currency of the organization.
Q10Leases (IFRS 16)Out of scope for phases 1 - 3; a lease payment is a financing / operating payment as the key already allows.
Q11Does the home go live first or the report first?Both in phase 1, but the data hook is written once and used by both (D1 and C2 share the RPC).
Q12Names in the UI"CapEx" and "OpEx" with tooltips; the organization may rename through the application texts.
Q13Who is the "investor"?A named, invited user with the read-only profile (H4), inside the organization. No public link, no anonymous access. A group-level investor sees several organizations through the PART 54 mechanism.
Q14Live or frozen for investors?Frozen: the investor pages read the snapshot of the last closed period (H6); a "live" switch exists only for the CFO and CEO.
Q15Company-wide ratios (CapEx intensity, CapEx / depreciation)Need the company context input (H2, revenue / EBITDA / depreciation per fiscal year, typed or CSV). The app never invents them from the project data.
Q16Which KPI definitions?The list of the investor strip above; each with ONE definition kept in a model file and shown in a modal; "management measure, not IFRS" in the footnote. The CFO may rename or hide a KPI per organization.
Q17Hide person-level cost from investors?Yes: rates, salaries and contract prices of persons are aggregated to the project / budget item level for the investor profile (H4); the time sheet shows hours only.

Risks and traps (from the project's own pitfall list)

  • Old budget items are all operating (PART 32 backfill). A CapEx report on unreviewed items is wrong → A8 first.
  • Freeze at Post. If the report reads the class from the live budget item, changing an item rewrites history and the report no longer agrees with the closed month → A3.
  • Two meanings of sumForAccounting (a document line = total with VAT, register = net). Every new money column must go through the VAT basis function, never add columns itself (kit8/pm/vat/README.md).
  • Demo numbers on the home consistent with each other are convincing until a CFO compares them with Monitor01 → D1 before any marketing use.
  • Consolidation twin lists (three places in PART 52) and permission matrix rows are easy to forget for a new table; Docker tests 53 / 100 will say so.
  • Read-only projects (PART 51): a new table of project data needs its row in the lists, a new automatic write must return quietly for a read-only project.
  • Scope creep into an ERP. Fixed asset accounting for statutory books is a different product; Q3 keeps the app on the project side.
  • Performance. Monitor09 reads four movement tables; use the monthly turnover / totals tables and a bench like bench_monitor01.sh, not raw movements for the screen.
  • Rate and VAT basis labels. A CFO accepts any basis if the screen says which one; D4 is therefore P0.
  • Live numbers in front of an investor. A number that changes between two openings of the same report destroys trust. H6 (snapshot) must exist before an investor profile is given to anyone (H4 after H6).
  • Project data is not company data. CapEx intensity and free cash flow of the company need revenue and operating cash flow the app does not hold. A tile that silently uses project revenue would mislead; label "project" vs "company" (H2, Q15).
  • Non-IFRS measures. If the numbers go into an investor publication, local rules on alternative performance measures may apply; the accountant confirms the wording of the definitions.
  • Confidentiality. An investor profile must not read salaries, rates or contract prices (Q17) and every read of it is logged; test with the permission matrix test pattern (Docker 53).
  • Good looks without data is the main reputational risk: a polished home on demo numbers is worse than a plain one on real numbers. Order: D1, then H12.

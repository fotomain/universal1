3 - The calendar lock

Your rule: the application must disable a change in the calendar when the resource is busy in that period - the capacity must not be less than the active business. I read "active business" as hours already booked by posted movements.

The invariant. For every resource R and every day D that has bookings: capacity(R, D) ≥ booked(R, D). Post keeps it from one side (it refuses to book above capacity, in capacityModeBlock mode); the calendar lock keeps it from the other (it refuses to lower capacity below what is booked).

3.1 Which changes are guarded

ChangeWhy it can break the invariant
A day row: hours lowered, day switched off, a vacation / sick day added, a day row deletedthat day's capacity falls
Fill (any mode, any year, "overwrite manual")writes many days at once
The pointer resourceCalendar of a person / equipment / contract changed or clearedthe resource gets another calendar, so another capacity on every day
Contract dates changed (contractStartDate / contractFinishDate) or a contract added or deletedmoves the days on which the contract's calendar has priority (section 2.4)
A calendar deletedrefused while any resource or contract points to it

3.2 The rule

  • For the affected range, compute new capacity of every resource that uses the calendar (through the priority of section 2.4) and compare with booked (posted movements of the whole organization, section 4).
  • Refuse when newCapacity < booked on any day and the change lowers the capacity of that day. A change that raises or keeps capacity is always allowed - so a day that is already over (a legacy booking, or capacityModeWarn mode) can still be repaired.
  • The message names the first conflict and counts the others: "Anna K. has 6 h booked on 2026-10-14 (task "Interface design"); the capacity cannot be less than 6 h."
  • Applies to the past too: hours booked on a past day are still booked.
  • It is always on, whatever resourceCapacityStrongMode says: bookings exist in every mode, and a calendar change is rarer than a Post.

3.3 Three layers

1 - the screen

Disable

The calendar screen asks a read-only function (organization_calendar_lock_info) for the locked days of the shown month. A locked day shows the lock icon, a tooltip with the reason, the hours cell is read-only and the off switch is disabled. The Fill menu runs the same function first and says what it would refuse.

2 - SQL triggers

Refuse

BEFORE INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE on managementCalendarDaysTable and a trigger on the four pointer tables call organization_calendar_change_conflicts(...) and RAISE EXCEPTION with the message above. Here a raise is right: nothing is to be kept. This is the guarantee; a client that skips the screen is still stopped.

3 - Fill

All or nothing

fill_management_calendar checks the whole year before it writes and refuses with the list of conflicts, rather than writing most of the year and skipping some days (decided, Q7).

3.3b The help button "!"

Q7 asks for a description of the logic for the user. A "!" button sits next to Fill on the calendar screen (and as a tooltip on a locked hours cell). Its text is written in plain words, in the pmT dictionaries like the other Finances texts:

Why can I not lower these hours?
A resource cannot work less than the hours already planned for it. Every posted task line of your organization that books Anna K. on a day is counted. If the hours of that day in this calendar become less than the planned hours, the change is refused - first move the task or reduce its hours, then change the calendar. You can always raise the hours. A Fill is refused as a whole if one day would break this; the list shows which days and which projects and tasks hold the hours. Days of the project calendar that are holidays can be copied into this calendar with "Follow project holidays".

3.4 Concurrency

A Post and a calendar change at the same moment must not both succeed. Both take the same advisory lock per resource key (section 7), in sorted order: the Post locks the resources of its lines, the calendar change locks every resource that uses the calendar. A change of the standard calendar (everybody uses it) would lock all resources of the organization for a moment; that is acceptable because it is a rare administrator action, and the lock is held only for the duration of the check.

3.5 Cost

The check is one set-based query: days in the range × resources using the calendar against booked per resource-day (an aggregate of the occupancy view limited to those resources and days). The pointers are found with the existing GIN index on rowJSON (rowJSON @> '{"resourceCalendar": "…"}'). A benchmark with a standard calendar used by 5 000 resources is in WP 12.3 before the lock is switched on for the standard calendar.

4 - What "busy" means

Day 2026-10-15      Resource: John (person)      Capacity from his calendar: 8 h

Task 101 (posted, project A)     [=========== 6 h ===========]
Task 102 (posting, project B)                [======= 4 h =======]       project A and B: same organization
Booked: 6 + 4 = 10 h  >  8 h  ->  BUSY by 2 h        (a day-grain check sees the 10 h, not the hours inside the day)

Capacity model recommended

booked(R,D) = Σ qty of every posted occupancy row of R on D, in the organization
busy(R,D) = booked(R,D) > capacityHours(R,D)

6 h + 2 h = exactly 8 h is not busy; the boundary is "greater than". A day with capacity 0 (weekend, vacation) and any booking is busy.

Exclusive model special case

"Either free or taken": for bookings A and B of one resource with half-open intervals [start, end): collision = A.start < B.end AND A.end > B.start. A flag on the resource's calendar (rowJSON.mode = 'exclusive'), not a second mechanism. Needs hour / minute data (Phase 2).

  • Counted: rows of the same resource key in projects with the same rowParentGUID, except the task being posted (Post deletes its old rows first); the lines of the posted task are checked against each other (E1).
  • Rows with null or zero quantity occupy nothing (⚠ "no hours", never refused for that). Only hour lines are comparable (E5).
  • Rows of lines with taskIsPosted = false are ignored (E3).

5 - The variants (answering "POSSIBLE VARIANTS")

recommended

A. Optimistic, inside Post

  2. In the same transaction, for every Time line: sum the other bookings of its resource in the organization and compare with the calendar capacity.

alternative

B. Pessimistic, check first

Calculate in memory and ask "is R free?" before writing.

For: nothing written and deleted. Against: two code paths; a race between check and write; pre-Correct033 numbers; the client would need colleagues' bookings. Useful only as the Phase 3 preview.

alternative

C. Sub-transaction (savepoint)

The check and insert sit in a PL/pgSQL BEGIN … EXCEPTION block; a conflict raises inside it and its rows vanish.

For: no explicit DELETE; fits "whole task" (Q6). Against: cannot drop one line and keep the others.

alternative

D. Constraint in the database

EXCLUDE USING gist on (resource, time range).

For: cannot be bypassed. Against: only the exclusive model; no "8 h per day". Optional hardening in Phase 2.

7 - The flow inside Post (variant A)

Post pressed→ client: calculate days (as today)→ create_task_line_plan→ lock task→ lock the resources of the Time lines (sorted)→ replace rows with planMovementStatus = false (Time lines) + Correct033→ busy check: capacity from the calendars→ busy: delete its rows, taskIsPosted = false→ the others: planMovementStatus = true→ timeline · answer with busy

The lock. Two users post two tasks of one person at once; each transaction sees only committed rows, so both would pass. One pg_advisory_xact_lock per resource key, taken in sorted order (no deadlock), held until commit. The calendar lock (section 3.4) takes the same keys.

Delete, or raise? A RAISE EXCEPTION rolls the whole transaction back, so a DELETE before it is lost too.
